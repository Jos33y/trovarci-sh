**In short:** SendBlaster Pro costs $129, and its latest version, 4.4.2, is from April 2020. If you want the same thing, a program you pay for once that sends through your own email account, the closest alternatives are **Trovarcis Sender**, **MaxBulk Mailer** and **Atomic Mail Sender**. If you only send from Gmail and don't mind a monthly fee, **Mailmeteor** and **GMass** work inside Gmail. **Mailchimp** suits newsletters, with your list kept on its servers.

## Why people look for a SendBlaster alternative {#why-switch}

- **No updates since 2020.** Version 4.4.2 came out in April 2020. Mail providers have tightened their rules a lot since then.
- **The free version is limited.** It sends to 100 recipients at a time, keeps 2 lists and can't send attachments, so real campaigns need the $129 Pro license.
- **Tracking goes through a cloud service.** SendBlaster's open and click reports run on its cloud tracking service, so tracking images are added to your emails.

## 1. Trovarcis Sender {#trovarcis-sender}

**Best for:** sending your own lists through accounts you already have, with nothing leaving your computer except the mail itself.

Trovarcis Sender is a Windows app you pay for once. It sends through your own SMTP accounts: Gmail, Google Workspace, Zoho, Amazon SES, SendGrid, Brevo, Mailgun, Postmark or your own server.

- **Private by design.** Your lists and your SMTP credentials are stored locally. Trovarcis never sees them, and Sender adds no tracking images to your emails.
- **Checks before it sends.** Typos, duplicates and broken addresses are set aside before anything goes out, and you can read the exact message each recipient will get.
- **Keeps each account inside its limits.** Set a daily cap per account and Sender spreads the campaign across your accounts, with pause, resume and sending hours.
- **A report in the server's own words** for every recipient who didn't get the email.

**Price:** $79 once until February 28, 2027, then $99. Use it on 3 computers, with every 1.x update included, and a 15-day refund if it doesn't work for you.

**Good to know:** Windows only for now. It doesn't track opens, by design.

## 2. MaxBulk Mailer {#maxbulk-mailer}

**Best for:** Mac users who want a desktop sender.

A long-running mail merge and bulk email program for Mac and Windows. It sends through your own mail server or an SMTP service, with personalization, attachments, test messages and a send-rate limit.

**Price:** $99.95 once, per user. **Good to know:** at nearly the price of SendBlaster Pro, it's the main option if you're on a Mac.

## 3. Atomic Mail Sender {#atomic-mail-sender}

**Best for:** sending without an SMTP account of your own.

A Windows sender from AtomPark. It can send through its built-in server or through your SMTP, and adds personalization, spin text and an unsubscribe wizard.

**Price:** $89.90 once, for one PC. Each extra PC needs its own license. **Good to know:** sending through a built-in server means mail goes from your computer's own connection, which many mail providers treat with suspicion. Most senders still use an SMTP account.

## 4. Mailmeteor {#mailmeteor}

**Best for:** small sends straight from Gmail or Outlook.

Mailmeteor runs as a Gmail add-on and a Google Sheets add-on, and sends through your own Gmail, Google Workspace or Outlook account.

**Price:** free for 50 emails a day, then $5.99 to $35.99 per user per month. **Good to know:** you pay every month, and Gmail's own daily limits apply: 500 a day on a personal account, 2,000 on Google Workspace.

## 5. GMass {#gmass}

**Best for:** cold outreach with follow-ups, inside Gmail.

GMass works inside Gmail in Chrome. It adds mail merge from Google Sheets, and follow-up sequences and A/B tests on its higher plans.

**Price:** from $29.95 a month, or $249 a year. **Good to know:** Gmail's daily limits apply here too, and the cost adds up over a year of use.

## 6. Mailchimp {#mailchimp}

**Best for:** designed newsletters to people who signed up.

A web platform with templates, automations and hosted sign-up forms. Your list lives on Mailchimp's servers and mail goes out from theirs.

**Price:** free up to 250 contacts and 500 emails a month, then a monthly fee that grows with your contact count. **Good to know:** you pay for every contact you keep, whether you email them that month or not.

## How to choose {#how-to-choose}

- **You want what SendBlaster does, but current:** Trovarcis Sender on Windows, MaxBulk Mailer on a Mac.
- **You only ever send from one Gmail account, a few hundred a day:** Mailmeteor.
- **You run cold outreach with follow-ups:** GMass.
- **You send designed newsletters to subscribers:** Mailchimp.

Whatever you pick, check your list and your domain before the first send. The free [Domain Checker](/domain) shows whether your domain is set up to send and whether it is on a blocklist.
