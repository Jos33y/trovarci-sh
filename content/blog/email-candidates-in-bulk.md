**In short:** keep your candidates in a spreadsheet with a column for each detail you'd mention (first name, role, city, a personal line), write the email once with those details as fields, and send each candidate their own copy from your own address. Openings to people already in your database and interview invitations can go out by the hundred. Headhunting people who never applied is cold email: keep it to 30 to 50 a day per mailbox, plain text, with a line written for that person.

## The spreadsheet that makes it personal {#sheet}

Export candidates from your recruiting software, LinkedIn notes or job board, and keep the columns you'd use in a real email:

| first_name | email | role | city | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priya | priya@example.com | Senior accountant | Toronto | your audit work at a Big Four firm |
| Tomás | tomas@example.com | Payroll lead | Madrid | |
| Hannah | hannah@example.com | Senior accountant | Toronto | the ERP migration you led |

The email is then written once:

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> I'm hiring a {{role}} in {{city}} and thought of you because of {{note | your experience}}.

Each candidate gets their own email with their details. A blank cell falls back to the words after the bar, so Tomás reads "because of your experience", never "because of ,".

## Sending a new job to your candidate pool {#openings}

These people know you: they applied before, or you've spoken. That's the email inboxes are happy to deliver.

- **Filter the sheet to the role**, so a payroll lead never gets the accountant job.
- **Attach the job description** as a PDF, or link to the posting.
- **Send during working hours** on weekdays. An email that arrives at 9:30 on a Tuesday gets read; one that arrives at 2 a.m. gets buried.
- **Mind the daily limit**: 500 people a day from a personal Gmail account, 2,000 from Google Workspace. Count your other email too.

## Headhunting passive candidates {#headhunting}

Someone who never applied didn't ask to hear from you. Gmail and Outlook judge those emails harder, and enough "Report spam" clicks can get your mailbox suspended.

- **30 to 50 a day per mailbox.** Reach more by adding mailboxes, not by sending faster.
- **Plain text, no attachment, one link at most.** Attachments on a first email from a stranger are filtered more often.
- **One line written for that person**, the note column above. It's the difference between a recruiter and a mass mailing.
- **Make it easy to say no**: "Not looking? Reply 'no' and I won't contact you again." Then keep your word.
- **Use a separate domain** for large-scale headhunting, like getnorthstar.com beside northstar.com, so outreach can never hurt the address your clients write to.

## Interview invitations and rejections {#interviews}

The same sheet works for the shortlist. Add an interview_time column and every invitation carries that candidate's slot. For the candidates you turn down, a rejection that uses their name and the role they applied for takes the same two minutes as a generic one, and they remember it.

Replies land in your normal inbox, one candidate at a time.
