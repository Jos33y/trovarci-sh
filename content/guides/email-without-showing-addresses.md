## 1. A few people: use BCC

Put your own address in **To** and everyone else in **BCC**. Each person sees only themselves and you. Fine for ten people; risky for three hundred.

## 2. Why BCC goes wrong with big groups

- **One slip shares everything.** Pasting into CC instead of BCC once shows every address to everyone. It happens to everyone eventually.
- **Spam filters distrust it.** One email to hundreds of hidden recipients looks like a mass mailing, and lands in spam or Promotions for many.
- **The cap.** Gmail stops at 500 recipients per email, and 500 a day on a personal account.
- **No names.** Everyone gets "Dear all".

## 3. A big group: one email per person

Send each person their own copy from your address. Nobody can see the others, reply-to-all doesn't exist, and each email can start with the person's name. It reads like a letter, so it's treated like one.

## Used CC by mistake?

1. **Don't send a follow-up to the same list** asking people to ignore it: that's a second exposure.
2. **Send a short apology** to each person individually, or by BCC.
3. **If you're in the EU or UK**, sharing addresses without consent can count as a data breach. Note what happened; your organization may need to record it.
